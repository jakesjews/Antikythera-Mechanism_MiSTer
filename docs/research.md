# What the core reproduces, and from where

The core is a working model of the Antikythera Mechanism as it is understood
in 2026. Everything the gears do comes from published tooth counts and period
relations; where the fragments are silent the most widely accepted
reconstruction is used and named here. `tools/mechanism.py` holds every number
below and generates the RTL tables, so the sources and the hardware cannot
drift apart.

## Gear train

Tooth counts are the CT-derived counts of Freeth et al., *Nature* 444 (2006),
Supplementary Notes 3, with the 2008 reassignment of axes n, o, p and q to the
Games and Callippic dials (Freeth, Jones, Steele and Bitsakis, *Nature* 454,
2008). The core advances every shaft by exact modular arithmetic on these
counts, so the ratios between the pointers are exactly the ratios of the teeth.

| Train | Gears | Turns per year |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mean Sun / date pointer | b1 (223, read also as 224), b2 64 | 1 |
| Sidereal Moon | b2 64 → c1 38, c2 48 → d1 24, d2 127 → e2 32 | 254/19 |
| Lunar apsides (e3 carrier) | b2 64 → l1 38, l2 53 → m1 96, m3 27 → e3 223 | 477/4237 (8.88 years per turn) |
| Lunar anomaly | e5 50 → k1 50 (pin) → k2 50 (slot) → e6 50, e1 32 → b3 32 | anomalistic month, 223/239 relation |
| Metonic pointer | b2 64 → l1 38, l2 53 → m1 96, m2 15 → n1 53 | 5/19 (five turns of the spiral in 19 years) |
| Games dial | n2 57 → o1 60 | 1/4, anticlockwise |
| Callippic dial | n3 15 → p1 60, p2 12 → q1 60 | 1/76 |
| Saros pointer | e3 223, e4 188 → f1 53, f2 30 → g1 54 | 940/4237 (four turns in 223 months) |
| Exeligmos dial | g2 20 → h1 60, h2 15 → i1 60 | one turn in three Saros cycles |
| Crank | a1 48 → b1 | one year per 223/48 turns |

The b1 count is uncertain between 223 and 224; it only changes the crank
ratio, and the menu lets you choose.

## Anomalies and the Cosmos display

The Moon's pin-and-slot (k1/k2) is modelled as Hipparchus' eccentric theory, as
Freeth et al. (2006) showed it is, with the eccentricity 5¼ parts in 60 of
Hipparchus' lunar model. The true Sun uses his solar eccentricity of 1/24 with
the apogee at Gemini 5½°. These match the devices described by Freeth and
Jones (2012) and the 2021 Cosmos reconstruction.

The planets follow Freeth, Higgon, Dacanalis, MacDonald, Georgakopoulou and
Wojcik, *Scientific Reports* 11 (2021), "A Model of the Cosmos in the ancient
Greek Antikythera Mechanism": period relations Venus 289 synodic cycles in 462
years and Saturn 427 in 442 (both read from the front cover inscription), with
Mercury 1513 in 480, Mars 133 in 284 and Jupiter 315 in 344 derived in the same
paper. Epicycle sizes are the Almagest values (Mercury 22;30, Venus 43;10,
Mars 39;30, Jupiter 11;30, Saturn 6;30 in sixtieths). The display order from
the centre is Moon, Mercury, Venus, Sun, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, date, and the
Dragon Hand shows the lunar nodes regressing five turns in 93 years (the
corrected 2021 ratio, 18.6 years). The Cosmos can be switched off in the menu
to show only what survives in the fragments: the Sun, Moon and date.

## Front dial

Aries begins at the top and longitudes increase clockwise, as in the 2021
reconstruction. The zodiac names and the parapegma index letters follow the
readings in Bitsakis and Jones, *Almagest* 7 (2016) via the standard summaries;
star phases are placed at approximate longitudes for the latitude of Greece.

The calendar ring is drawn as the Egyptian civil year of 365 days with its
month names, which is what the surviving ring fragment (ΠΑΧΩΝ, ΠΑΥΝΙ, ΕΠΙΦΙ)
shows. Hole counts of the ring's locating holes point to 354 (Budiselic et
al. 2020; Woan and Bayley, *Horological Journal* 2024, 354–355 with 365 "not
plausible"; Malin and Dickens 2023). The menu therefore also offers a 354-day
lunar ring, registered to the New Moon as those papers propose. The ring is
registered automatically (one day every four years, as the operator of the
real instrument would have done) unless you turn it by hand.

## Back dials

Metonic months carry the Corinthian month names identified by Freeth et al.
(2008) and attributed to Epirus by Iversen, *Hesperia* 86 (2017). Years 1, 3,
6, 9, 11, 14 and 17 of the 19-year cycle have thirteen months; the repeated
month is Machaneus, the only intercalation attested on the dial (year 11,
cells 128–129, per Jones 2017). This layout matches every surviving cell
reading. The Games dial sectors are year 1 Isthmia and Olympia, year 2 Nemea
and Naa, year 3 Isthmia and Pythia, year 4 Nemea and Halieia, running
anticlockwise. The Exeligmos dial adds 0, 8 or 16 hours.

The Saros dial's eclipse glyphs are generated by the Eclipse Year Model of
Freeth, *PLoS ONE* 9 (2014): 38 units per month, node points at 66 and 289
units, lunar possibilities within 20 units of a node point, solar within 20
north or 7 south, and the consecutive-month rule. That yields the paper's 38
lunar and 28 solar predictions in 51 cells and reproduces every surviving
glyph. Hours are the inscribed values for the 22 preserved glyph times; the
remaining hours are computed from the Mechanism's own syzygies in seasonal
hours and are labelled RECONSTRUCTED on screen.

## Epochs

* **205 BC** (default): the Saros dial starts with the New Moon of 28 April
  205 BC (Carman and Evans 2014; Freeth 2014), and the Corinthian calendar and
  Games cycle start four lunar months later with the New Moon of 23 August
  205 BC (Iversen 2017; Jones, *ISAW Papers* 17, 2020). The Saros cells begin
  two eclipse-year units (first crescent) after conjunction.
* **178 BC**: Voulgaris, Mouratidis and Vossinakis (2022) place the start on
  22/23 December 178 BC, with the Metonic pointer at cell 1, the Saros pointer
  at cell 2, the Sun at the first day of Capricorn and Athyr 18, and the Games
  pointer in year 3.

Positions of the Sun, Moon, apogee, nodes and planets at each epoch come from
the mean elements in Meeus, *Astronomical Algorithms*, evaluated at the mean
New Moon. Dates are proleptic Julian with civil days starting at local
midnight in north-west Greece.

## Not modelled

Gear backlash, manufacturing error and jamming (Szigety and Arenas 2025 argue
the measured tooth errors would have jammed the real instrument; Edmunds'
measurements may overstate them), the conjectural inner day-counting dial of
the 2021 model, the lost planetary gear trains themselves, the excluded-day
scheme for 29-day months, and the Voulgaris et al. 2025–2026 proposals for a
365-division zodiac ring and extra indicator dials on b1.
